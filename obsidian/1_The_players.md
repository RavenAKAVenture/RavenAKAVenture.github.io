You take on the role of agents recruited by Briala. You’ll travel through the Eluvians, entering the crossroads, to appear in a variety of places across Southern Thedas and beyond; In fighting a guerilla war, you’ll undertake many of the activities a scoundrel would, but for entirely different reasons.

You’re playing revolutionaries; heroes, defying the odds. You’re not required to play elves, though that is the easiest narrative path. If you don’t, explaining why you’ve made this your fight—and how you gained Briala’s trust—is a necessary question to answer. Maybe you’re a human with an elven friend or lover to tie you to the cause; Maybe you’re a disgraced Orlesian noble with a serious bone to pick, and you want to see the entire system burn. Maybe you’re a dwarf, exiled from Orzammar, and all too familiar with oppressive regimes, ready to bring the fight in a way you couldn’t underground. Maybe you’re a mercenary who fell in with the rebellion after a chance encounter, who has proven their trustworthiness. Maybe you were sprung from prison, saved from a sentence(rightful or otherwise) in a mass jailbreak orchestrated by one of Briala’s agents.

You don’t need to be a true, ardent believer, but one way or another your fate is tied to this rebellion. Your responsibility is to make a character willing to fight for it.

**The Characters**

The dragon age games have a tradition, and this ruleset will stick to it. Your playbook will be a specialization associated with a base class. You have all the abilities and items of the base class alongside your specialization.

- **Warriors**: the archetypal fighter. Frontliners and durable whichever way you cut it, though there’s plenty of room for interpretation—a defender, wielding sword and shield? Or something more aggressive, dual wielding, maybe, or a two-handed weapon.
- **Rogues:** A little narrower here than the broad idea of scoundrels in Blades; This is a rogue in the dnd tradition. Stealthy and bringing knives, a bow, and cheap tricks to the fight.
- **Mages:** Have quite a lot distinguishing them in the setting, when held up to other mainline fantasy wizard equivalents. There will be the spellcasting you expect, but mages in Thedas have a difficult lot, and must be wary of possession by demons.
