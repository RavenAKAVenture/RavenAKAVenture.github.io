Blades is dark, gritty. It’s about Scoundrels. Dragon Age is arguably high fantasy(though it started out much more grounded)—but its tone has highs and lows. Humor has always been a part of its formula, but its better placed and used than in, say, a marvel movie. More often than not, the tone is handled well, and the heavy moments land. It’s high fantasy, yes, with heroes, villains, and a vibrant world; It’s also a series that contains compelling tragedy. Your player character can die at the end of Origins, and Dragon Age 2 is a tragedy no matter what you do. Unfortunately, Inquisition dropped the ball, but it had potential.

The writers being by and large white liberals from Canada with unexamined privilege compromises its thematic potential, but if you so choose, you can still wring out some hope from the setting. So that’s what I’m gonna try and do, because I am unwell in the head.

**Setting**

The world of dragon age is called Thedas.

Here is the absolute, most necessary, bare bones context for the campaign you’ll be playing. (Spoilers: its still a lot. Also, this is a recounting of _common wisdom_; what the people of the world know, not accounting for deep lore reveals) Ancient history is thus: There weren’t any humans on the continent. Deep beneath the ground the dwarves made their empire, and on the land above, the elves, immortal, created the empire of Elvhenan. When humans arrived, they founded the Tevinter Imperium. Human contact with the elves caused what the elves dubbed the quickening—they began to age, just as the humans did. Understandably, they retreated, closing their borders, until the cold tensions between them erupted into war. The Imperium would go on to win that war, annihilating the elven capital of Arlathan and subjugating the elves as slaves.

Humanity spread across the continent, splintering into many nations. Then came the blight. Conventional wisdom says it was the result of the Imperium’s Magister’s hubris. One way or another, the supernatural tide of evil ravaged the land for a century before its eventual end, weakening the Imperium. Eventually, a prophet rose—Andraste. She led an army against the Imperium from the south, and though she was betrayed and burned at the stake a martyr, the Imperium would never truly recover. The Chantry that spread in Andraste’s wake would shape the continent more than anything else in the centuries to come.

The elves, for their part, rose up against their masters. Shartan was their leader, and he agreed to fight alongside Andraste. For this, his people were promised a new homeland, and though Andraste would be slain, her husband Maferath would keep that promise. Free, the elves would march south, and there found the kingdom of the Dales.

Not two centuries would pass before the human empire of Orlais went to war with the Dales. The Chantry declared an Exalted March; in the end, the Dales could not stand against this crusade. The elves lost their homeland—again--and had to choose one of two fates: submit again to human rule, being forced into the slums and ghettos as second class citizens, or flee, living outside human society as nomads, their numbers small. Those taking the latter path styled themselves the Dalish, and distinguished themselves with facial tattoos, vallaslin_._ These two groups, city elves and dalish, are the main groups of elves in modern Thedas.

That, finally, brings us to the present. Orlais has fallen into civil war; the empress fights to hold onto her throne from the grand duke, a military-minded man with expansionist dreams. In the middle of this is Briala, an elven revolutionary seeking to weaken the human empire and improve the lot of her much maligned people. Though by all metrics the underdog, she has an ace up her sleeve: The eluvians. A recently awakened network of ancient magical mirrors that allow swift travel all across Thedas. Using these, she has struck behind enemy lines, raided supplies, and continually harried both human factions.

But now, the Chantry’s entire standing army—the templars--has gone rogue as the mage rebellion spreads. Suddenly, the chaos is deeper than anyone could have anticipated; as is the opportunity greater. Suddenly, a resistance isn’t all that Briala can enact.

It’s time for a war of liberation. If Orlais can be defeated, there will be no Exalted March, no retribution waiting in store. The Chantry’s house is on fire.

If Orlais can be defeated, the Dales could be free again.
